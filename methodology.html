How we researched and ranked AI contract review software

Last updated: November 9, 2026

What we did

  1. Built a long list. We collected AI contract review products named in current 2026 buyer's guides, market reports and legal-technology news.
  2. Checked each vendor's own site. On November 9, 2026 we read each vendor's official product, pricing and security pages and recorded what they say they offer and charge. One site (Sirion) blocked our automated reader. For Sirion we relied on its own pages as relayed by our search tool, and the listing says so.
  3. Checked current status. We looked for acquisitions, rebrands and shutdowns using press releases and legal-industry reporting. As a result we excluded Robin AI, whose business was broken up in late 2025 and early 2026. We also noted that Gavel was acquired by Relativity in June 2026, that ContractPodAi now operates as Leah, that Evisort is part of Workday, and that Lexion is part of Docusign.
  4. Ranked by fit for the query. 'AI contract review software' means software that analyzes and redlines contracts against standards. We ranked on: depth of pre-signature review (issue spotting, playbooks, redlines); how easily the intended buyer can adopt it; breadth of verified capabilities; transparency on pricing, trials and data handling; and evidence that the company is stable and the product current.

What we did not do

  • We did not test these products hands-on and we make no hands-on testing claims.
  • We did not run accuracy benchmarks. Accuracy, time-saving and customer-count figures on this site are vendor-reported and labeled as such.
  • We do not publish reader reviews or star ratings.

Limits of this approach

Vendor sites are marketing material. Most vendors hide their prices, and features can depend on your plan. A pilot on your own contracts is the only reliable way to judge quality. Rankings are editorial judgments about fit, not scientific scores.

Updates and corrections

We re-check vendor pages and status when we update the guide, and change the 'Last updated' date. If a fact is wrong or out of date, the vendor's official site takes precedence.
